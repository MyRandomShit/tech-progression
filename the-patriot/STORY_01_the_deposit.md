# Story Option 01 — *The Deposit*

> **Engine:** He takes a job-order inspector post inside the district engineering office that his mother's killers use as a laundry, and spends four years identifying them from a plastic chair in a room with bad aircon. Every reform in the book is a side effect of a move he made for the hunt.
>
> **Register:** Provincial procedural. Small towns, paperwork, grief, and — three times — a hole in the ground.

---

## Logline

A field technician robs his hometown bank to pay for his mother's transplant, walks out with a ledger that names forty years of theft but not one living man, and — after they kill her anyway — gets himself hired as a ₱610-a-day materials inspector in the district engineering office where the money still moves, so he can learn their names from the inside. The province becomes the best-governed in the country by accident, while he is busy deciding which of them has to disappear.

## Act I — The robbery, the ledger, the murder

The deposit is ₱1.4M against ₱310K in savings and eleven days on the Cebu consortium's calendar.

He does not plan a robbery; he plans a **withdrawal**. His employer services the branch's delivery vans, which gives him a badge, a service window, and nobody's attention. He notices — the way he notices everything, without knowing why he is right — that the vault's time-lock arbitration falls back to a local clock when the sync link drops, and that the link drops in weather.

He takes ₱1.4M exactly and leaves a handwritten count sheet.

In Box 114, unlocked, he finds **the Deposit Book**: four hundred pages, six hands, 1987–2038 — the reconciliation ledger for four decades of provincial theft. Flood control, right-of-way, calamity procurement, confidential funds. The buckets machines cannot read, which is where the money went after transparency won everything else.

Nenita dies nine days later in a ward, of a medication event that the hospital's own honest audit trail records honestly, because the hospital is honest and the nurse was paid.

**And the Deposit Book is useless.** Two reasons, and both matter:

1. In 2038 a perfect forgery of a 1991 ledger costs forty pesos of compute. A document without provenance is a rumor with good handwriting.
2. **It names the dead.** The Book's last legible attributions are from 2016. Everyone above ₱50M in it has retired, died, or been promoted into abstraction. It tells him a laundry exists and where the pipe enters the ground. It does not tell him who is standing on the pipe *today*.

He needs the current names. The current names are not written down anywhere. They are only *in the building*.

---

## Act II — Infiltration

Six months after the funeral he takes a **job order contract as a materials inspector**, ₱610 a day, no benefits, renewable every six months — the most disposable position in the Philippine civil service and therefore the easiest to enter and the least likely to be examined.

He is now the man who stands in the sun watching a pour and signs a form nobody reads.

**How he finds them:** `Trace`, entirely, and he thinks it is diligence. He walks a completed road and knows which section was short by ninety millimetres. He watches a bid opening and knows which two envelopes were written by the same person. He shakes a hand at a Christmas party and knows the man is downstream of a room he has never been in. He assumes any competent inspector would see this. Nobody else sees any of it, and he never once wonders why.

In eleven months he builds **the board**: sixty-one names, five tiers, drawn on the back of a rice-sack in the room he rents above a hardware store.

- **Tier 5** — bagmen, drivers, two guards, a courier. Replaceable.
- **Tier 4** — section chiefs, three contractors, a COA resident auditor who signs and looks away.
- **Tier 3** — Engr. Sarmiento. The provincial administrator. A bank branch manager.
- **Tier 2** — the Fund's fixer, a man he can only describe as *a person who has been in the room where his mother's file was opened.*
- **Tier 1** — blank for two years.

## Act III — The moves, and what they accidentally did

**This is the engine of the book. He never once acts to improve anything. He acts to get closer, and the province gets better behind him like a wake.**

| # | What he needed | What he did | What it accidentally fixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Access to pre-2030 project files locked in the District Engineer's anteroom | Fed a real, verifiable finding on a 2034 slope protection contract to a citizen-audit collective through four cutouts. Sarmiento is suspended, not charged | **Marilou Bataller becomes OIC.** Within two years the district's post-typhoon road failure rate drops 61%. Forty thousand people in four towns get a highway that survives October. Eli's actual motive: Bataller signs his file access requests without reading them |
| 2 | To see which contractors were bidding in concert | Pushed a dull "bid document standardization" reform through the new OIC as a workload complaint from inspectors | Adopted region-wide within 18 months. Regional procurement savings estimated at ₱2.1B over four years. He wanted the envelopes to be comparable. That is all he wanted |
| 3 | A protected line of sight into the provincial capitol's disbursements | Arranged for a genuinely incorruptible COA auditor — a woman he identified from the Book's own list of *people who were reassigned for refusing* — to be requested for the province | She uncovers eleven schemes that have nothing to do with him and destroys three careers he had no interest in. He is mildly inconvenienced by the attention |
| 4 | Sarmiento's replacement to survive the counterattack | Spent four months quietly making Bataller unremovable: leaked her performance data to the swarm, got her a national award, made her too visible to fire | She is now a public figure. In year six she is appointed Regional Director and the reform spreads to six provinces. **He does not attend the ceremony. He is in Iloilo, following a driver** |
| 5 | The Fund's fixer to surface | Broke the laundry's *throughput*, deliberately — over eight months he made the district's paperwork so clean that the pipe backed up | The fixer comes in person. This is the only move in the book he makes on purpose, and its side effect is that the province stops being stealable, permanently, because the clean paperwork outlives the reason for it |

**The tonal rule:** every one of these gets one paragraph from a stranger's POV — a mother whose evacuation road held, a bidder who won honestly and doesn't know why, a nurse who got paid on time — and Eli is never in those paragraphs and never learns.

## The dark ledger

Four years. Three holes.

| Who | What happened | What it cost him |
|---|---|---|
| **A courier**, 31 | Eli identifies him as the man who carried the instruction to the ward. He does not kill him. He gives him to the Fund — leaks that the courier has been skimming, which is true — and the Fund handles it, cleanly and privately, the way they handled his mother. **He uses their own protocol on them.** | The first night he sleeps eight hours since the funeral, and understands what that means about him, and does not stop |
| **A section chief**, 44 | Genuinely needs to vanish — he is three days from identifying Eli, and there is no version where he is bought or beaten. Eli does it himself, badly, in a quarry access road in Panay, and it takes much longer than he expected | Physically sick for a week. Files an inspection report the following Monday. The report is excellent |
| **Bgy. Kagawad Orlando Vergara**, 52 | **The wrong man.** Eli reads him as downstream of the ward — and he is, because he drove his own sister to that hospital that week, and `Trace` reads provenance, not intent. Vergara disappears. Nine months later Eli understands. Vergara had four children | The center of the book. He does not confess, does not turn himself in, does not stop. He starts, quietly, paying for the youngest one's schooling through a cutout, and this is somehow worse than everything else |

Vergara is the reason the book cannot be triumphant. Everything Eli achieves afterward is real and it is standing on a man who drove his sister to a hospital.

## Ending

Tier 1 is not a person. It is the Fund's standing protocol — a paragraph in a manual, written in 1991 by a man who died in 2004, that says a compromised custody site requires removal of the opener's leverage. His mother was killed by a *sentence*. There is nobody to kill.

He is arrested for the robbery, which he did. He pleads to it. He refuses the defense that would require the Book.

Capiz, when he leaves it, has the lowest infrastructure-anomaly index in the Republic and a regional director who is about to become a national figure, and none of it has his name near it, and he does not think about it much.

## `Trace` in this option

**How it's seeded:** He is the only inspector in Region VI whose findings are never overturned. Over four years this becomes a joke, then a legend, then a problem — his reputation is the reason Tier 2 finally comes to look at him.

**The nosebleeds** start in year two, always after long days on site.

**Chapter 9, two paragraphs, never mentioned again:** a Cedula field team runs a sweep through Roxas looking for something else entirely. The operator notes a null reading at the district engineering office — an absence where the interference math says there should be a person — logs it as equipment drift, and leaves. **They walk past him in the canteen.**

**The reveal:** year four, in a Registry holding room, from a corrupted Cedula Architect who is there for an unrelated reason and cannot stop looking at him. She explains what he is in about six minutes. She is not kind about it. She is not cruel either — she is a woman who cannot remember her father's face explaining the invoice to a man who has not yet read his.

**The knife:** he tells her he can't remember his mother's face and has always assumed it was grief.

She says: *No.*

**The last line territory:** he goes home to Roxas and fixes the elementary school's solar controller, because it broke again, because things break — and he watches his own hands do it and cannot tell anymore which parts of him are him.

## Grounding notes

- Job order / contractual status: no benefits, six-month renewals, the invisible tier of Philippine government employment. This is the whole reason infiltration is *plausible*.
- DPWH district office texture: the covered court, the canteen, the Christmas party, the tarpaulin with the district engineer's face on every project.
- Flood control, right-of-way, calamity procurement, confidential funds — the four buckets where money hides in 2038.
- COA resident auditors, bid opening rituals, the physical envelope.
- Hospital deposit-before-admission arithmetic. Dialysis three times a week and what it does to a household's Tuesdays.
- Post-Mandanas devolution taken to 2038: provinces with real budgets, real theft, and citizen auditors fighting province by province.
