# Story Option 03 — *The Swarm*

> **Engine:** Financial thriller. He doesn't leak the file — he *trades* on it, and spends the winnings building the things the stolen money was supposed to build. Then ten million amateur investors start reverse-engineering the anonymous fund with impossible timing.
>
> **Register:** Propulsive, contemporary, money. The most commercial of the five. Reads fast, hurts late.

---

## Logline

A grid engineer robs a provincial bank to fly his nine-year-old daughter to a cure, takes home a ledger of who actually owns the Philippines, loses her anyway to a customs hold that was a phone call — and turns the most valuable secret in the country into an anonymous fund that quietly builds water plants and cancer centers, until the nation's own army of hobbyist investors starts hunting the ghost beating the market by eleven percent a year.

---

## Cast

| | |
|---|---|
| **Protagonist** | **Mateo "Teo" Villarba**, 41. Grid systems engineer for a Visayas cooperative — batteries, substations, typhoon restoration. Off-days: corporate watcher. He is *known*, faintly, in the watch communities as the guy who reads satellite imagery of factory lots for fun and is never wrong about inventory. |
| **X** | **Beatriz "Bea" Villarba**, 9, his daughter. Relapsed leukemia. The public system funds standard care to a genuinely good standard. It does not fund the third-line CAR-T therapy, which is manufactured in Osaka, priced in yen, and shipped cryogenic with a 72-hour viability window. |
| **The wife** | **Grace Villarba** — alive. She leaves him in Year 2 and she is right to. The marriage is the book's honest cost. |
| **The keepers** | **The Register** — not a committee, a *class*. Eleven families and the law firm that has maintained their nominee structures since 1946. Their enforcement is a phone call to a friend, which is worse than a gun and cheaper. |
| **The mirror** | **"kalabaw_quant"** — the swarm's best analyst, 26, works from a township in Iloilo, has never met anyone. Spends four years hunting Teo out of pure sport, and is the only person in the country good enough to find him. |

## Act 0 — What he is before

Teo is the 2038 Filipino everyman taken to his logical extreme: a working professional with four days off a week who filled the void with an obsession. His obsession is **reading the world through its exhaust** — port activity, job postings, satellite lot counts, drone-corridor filings, substation load curves. He does not trade much. He watches because watching is the game.

His genius is that he treats every system — the grid, the market, a hospital's billing, a barangay's water table — as the same object. He has never articulated this. He would tell you he's a lineman with a hobby.

Bea inherits it. She counts things. At seven she tells him the sari-sari store is running out of eggs on Wednesdays, and she's right, and it's the proudest he's ever been.

---

## The robbery

Cash still moves physically in the island provinces, and by 2038 it moves by **drone** — domestic airframes, cooperative-operated corridors, cash-in-transit runs between rural branches on published windows.

Teo has been reading drone corridor filings for six years for fun. He knows the reconciliation window between a branch's vault-out event and the corridor's custody-in handshake is four minutes and depends on a link that fails in weather. He does not hack anything. He **schedules a storm** — he files a legitimate cooperative maintenance advisory that reroutes power to a relay, degrades the link on a day the weather will do the rest, and walks into a rural Cebu branch during the four minutes when the money belongs to nobody.

₱9.4M. He takes ₱9.4M and leaves ₱40M, and this is the detail that later convinces one very smart 26-year-old that the thief is a person with an *invoice*, not an appetite.

In the vault, in the branch's dead-records cabinet — because a bank that has swallowed four other banks since 1998 inherits their garbage — he finds a maroon binder.

---

## The document — the Book of Positions

Not crimes. **Ownership.**

By 2038 everything about a Philippine listed company is public: filings, margins, earnings calls with hobbyist watch parties, supply chains reconstructed from orbit. Everything except the last mile — **who is actually behind the nominee chains.** Beneficial ownership is the one opaque layer left, and it is opaque because it is the layer where politics and capital touch.

The Book maps it. Eleven families. Four hundred corporate vehicles. Nominee-of-nominee structures running back to a 1946 holding company and, before that, to a 1901 one. It reconciles political office against equity position across ninety years.

He cannot publish it. Three reasons, all of them true:

1. **Forgery is free in 2038.** An anonymous ownership map is a rumor with good typography.
2. **Thirty percent of Filipinos own equities now.** A disorderly repricing of the eleven families' holdings doesn't punish the eleven families. It vaporizes the retirement of a tricycle driver in Naga who bought his first shares in 2035 because the app made it easy.
3. **It would not change anything.** The structures are legal. That is the entire point of them.

But it is the most perfect trading edge that has ever existed, and Teo is a man who reads the world through its exhaust.

---

## The murder

The Osaka therapy is arranged. Paid — with the ₱9.4M. Manufactured from Bea's own cells. Shipped.

It sits in a cryogenic container at NAIA cargo for six days on a documentary hold, because a customs examiner received a call about the classification of an unlisted biologic, and the caller was a friend of a friend, and the friend of a friend is on page 88 of the Book.

Nobody threatens Teo. Nobody visits. The Register does not do that. Somebody simply *asked a question*, and the machine did the rest, and by the time the hold lifts, the cells are dead and so, four months later, is Bea.

**The horror of this book is that the murder weapon is a phone call, and no crime was committed, and everyone involved is a decent person doing their job.**

---

## The turn — why he keeps it

He drafts the leak. He gets as far as the upload.

He stops because of the arithmetic he cannot stop doing: publishing costs the tricycle driver ₱180,000 and costs the eleven families a bad quarter and a legal retainer. **Transparency, weaponized clumsily, is regressive.** He has spent six years learning exactly how markets punish the people who cannot afford lawyers.

So instead: *if I know what everyone else will eventually find out, I don't need them to find out. I only need to be early.*

## The ladder — how a country improves at eleven percent a year

**Rung 1 — The fund (Year 1).**
He opens accounts. He is not greedy and he is not fast; he takes small positions with impossible timing, on the *hollow* side — the firms whose real owners are extracting rather than building, whose collapse is arithmetic. He is never wrong because he isn't predicting. He's reading the answer key.

₱9.4M becomes ₱200M in fourteen months. He does not buy anything for himself. He still drives the cooperative truck.

**Rung 2 — The build (Year 2–4).**
He starts spending it on the physical country, anonymously, through a lattice of small foundations and cooperative loans:

- A municipal water treatment plant in a Samar township that the 2029 flood-control funds were supposed to build and didn't.
- Nineteen kilometres of rail spur nobody would finance because the ROI ran forty years.
- A regional pediatric oncology center in Cebu. Nobody's name on it. Not hers. **Especially** not hers.
- Boring things: cold chain. A regional cold chain, warehouse by warehouse, so that nothing dies in cargo again.

He is, by Year 4, the largest private funder of public goods in the history of the Republic, and there is no name attached to any of it, and the LGUs who receive it assume it's a foreign donor.

**Rung 3 — The repricing (Year 4–5).**
Here is the accidental part, and it is the best idea in this option:

Teo's fund is short the hollow companies. As his positions grow, the *market* starts to notice — not him, but the anomaly of persistent, patient, correct pressure against eleven specific conglomerates. Capital is a herd animal. Analysts build models to explain the pressure. Watch communities build theories. And in trying to explain the ghost, **ten million amateurs independently reconstruct the beneficial-ownership map he refused to publish** — not from his file, but from the shape of his trades.

He didn't leak the truth. He made the truth *inferable*, and the swarm did the rest, with real provenance, real methodology, real receipts.

**Rung 4 — The law (Year 5–6).**
Once the swarm can see nominee chains, capital reprices political risk. Being an oligarch stops being a discount and starts being a *cost of capital*. The families' vehicles trade at persistent, structural haircuts. Their own boards begin unwinding the structures for shareholder reasons.

The beneficial ownership disclosure act, when it finally passes, is uncontroversial and boring and supported by the people it destroys, because by then the market has already priced it in and the only remaining value of the secret is the shame.

## The hunters

**The Register** hunts him with lawyers, regulators, and friends — legitimate AMLC flags, a tax investigation that is entirely correct, a leak to the press about a grid engineer with a dead daughter and an unexplained ₱9.4M. Every attack is true.

**The swarm** hunts him for fun, and the swarm is worse. Ten million people with four free days and better tooling than the SEC, running the exact craft Teo taught himself, pointed at him. **kalabaw_quant** gets there first — not through the trades, but through the *gifts*: she notices that nineteen unrelated infrastructure projects in five provinces share a nonstandard specification for battery enclosure grounding, and only one man in the country specifies grounding that way, and he wrote it in a cooperative maintenance manual in 2033 under his own name.

His genius is his fingerprint. He is undone by the one thing he could not stop doing correctly.

---

## Ending

Cornered — regulators closing, the Register's lawyers three weeks from a freeze that will strand ₱9B mid-deployment — he does the only irreversible thing available.

He publishes **everything except the Book**: his complete position history, his full methodology, his models, his sources, the entire architecture of the edge, timestamped and verifiable, and then dissolves the fund into eleven thousand cooperative accounts he does not control.

He gives away the alpha. Every watcher in the country can now run it. The repricing that would have taken a decade takes nine weeks, and it is orderly, because it is *distributed* — the tricycle driver in Naga is on the right side of it, because he got the model at the same moment the hedge funds did.

Teo goes to prison for the robbery, which he did, and for tax evasion, which he also did. He refuses to explain the Book, which is never found. He is not a hero in the press; he is a complicated man with a dead child and nine billion pesos of other people's questions.

**Last scene:** the pediatric oncology center in Cebu, five years later, from the point of view of a mother who has never heard of Mateo Villarba. The building has a name on it. It is the name of a barangay.

## The Zero layer

1. **The dead-zone map.** Teo's satellite-exhaust hobby produces, as a byproduct, an anomaly index — nineteen regions where his models are persistently, slightly wrong. Optical returns off by a fraction. Physics not quite closing. He assumes sensor calibration drift and files it as noise in a public dataset.

   They are **dead zones** — regions drained by heavy daemon usage (`WORLD_LORE.md` §IV). A Natural with a laptop has accidentally produced the first independent survey of the coder world's damage, and published it, and nobody noticed. **The Cedula noticed.** Two chapters of quiet, unexplained attention from people who are not regulators and not lawyers, who ask him one strange question and then never return.

2. **The 1901 shareholder.** The Book's root entity, incorporated 1901, lists a shareholder of record who also appears — same name, same notation — in the 1946 reconstitution, the 1972 reorganization, and the 2011 restructure. The families assume it's a family name passed down. It's an **arc**. (The Epoch, `WORLD_LORE.md` §VI.6.) They have been in the Philippine ownership structure since the Americans arrived, and their instruction to their local segment has never changed: *the system must not crash.* Teo's orderly, distributed repricing is, to the ring, an acceptable outcome. **The Epoch lets him win**, and neither he nor the reader is ever told this outright.

3. **Bea.** She counted things. She was seven and she was right about the eggs. The most quietly devastating implication in the option: she was latent too, and she died before anything could wake her, and Zero's seam — the gift that would eventually give the world Linus — passed through a nine-year-old in Cebu and out the other side without touching anything.

## Risks / weaknesses

- Finance can go opaque fast. Every market beat needs a kitchen-table consequence within a page.
- The "he gives away the alpha" climax is the best ending of the five but it is *conceptual* — it needs a physical, human scene running underneath it or it will read as a press release.
- Grace leaving must not be a subplot. Either it's load-bearing or cut it.

## Open questions

- Does he ever identify the man who made the customs call? (Recommend: yes, Year 3 — and the man is a mid-level examiner who has genuinely forgotten. Teo stands in his kitchen and cannot do it.)
- Does kalabaw_quant turn him in, or protect him? (Recommend: turns him in, publicly, correctly — and then spends the rest of her life running his models for free.)
- Where does the Book end up?
