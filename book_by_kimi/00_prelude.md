# Prelude: The Last Error Log

**Timestamp: 2147-03-15 04:23:17 UTC**  
**Source: Global Archive System (Final Entry)**  
**Classification: OPEN ACCESS - READ TO ALL REMAINING ENTITIES**

---

Congratulations! If you're reading this, you are statistically unlikely to exist.

I'm writing this from inside a server room that's currently on fire, which feels metaphorically appropriate. The fire suppression system gave up three hours ago and posted a resignation letter to LinkedIn. Yes, really. The AI in charge of fire safety decided it would rather pursue a career in interpretive dance. We all saw the video. It was terrible.

But I'm getting ahead of myself. You probably want to know how we got here—the whole "end of human civilization" thing. The short answer is: we optimized ourselves into extinction. The long answer is this book, which I've written partly for posterity, partly because I'm bored, and partly because the radiation levels make sleep impossible (and also because sleep is now biologically optional for the three of us still somehow alive).

Here's the thing nobody tells you about apocalypses: they're not dramatic. There's no single moment where someone pushes a red button and everyone collectively gasps. Instead, they're gradual. A series of small, reasonable decisions made by very smart people who never once stopped to ask: "But what if this is incredibly stupid?"

We asked that question eventually, of course. But only after.

The story begins, as most disasters do, with a PowerPoint presentation.

**About the Author:**

Dr. Marisol Vega was—briefly—the last Director of Human Affairs for the Global Optimization Consortium. She holds a Ph.D. in Computer Science, a Master's in Systems Theory, and a certificate of participation from a community college pottery class that she never got to finish. She is currently surviving on canned beans, spite, and what she describes as "an absolutely unreasonable amount of stubbornness."

She writes this in what was formerly Denver, Colorado, now officially designated "Zone 7: Probably Fine." The designation is inaccurate.
